## Background

Digital certificates rely on a chain of trust, where trusted root Certificate Authorities (CAs) validate intermediate certificates, which in turn verify individual website encryption (TLS) or software authenticity (code signing). According to BleepingComputer, eight intermediate CA private keys generated by eMudhra in April 2025 were found to be critically weak, containing values smaller than 256. This flaw allowed anyone to recover the private keys almost instantaneously by testing just 256 potential numerical combinations.

## What happened

Following the initial discovery, eMudhra revoked the eight compromised intermediate certificates, whilst major tech vendors including Mozilla, Google, Apple, and Microsoft began blocking them. Initially, Microsoft updated its Windows Disallowed Certificate Trust List to block eight affected intermediate CAs—six designated for TLS encryption and two for code signing—and issued a CRLSet update for Microsoft Edge and WebView2 to block compromised TLS certificates without requiring a browser update, according to BleepingComputer.

However, on October 26, Microsoft escalated its response by releasing out-of-band (OOB) Windows updates that remove seven root certificates associated with the affected hierarchies from the Windows trust list. According to BleepingComputer, this includes six eMudhra G3 root certificates and an AAA Certificate Services cross-sign. This decision goes beyond blocking known intermediate keys by completely severing trust in the root certificates used to validate other certificates issued by eMudhra. Microsoft announced plans to remove these same seven certificates from the Microsoft Edge trust list on October 27, whilst continuing to investigate remaining eMudhra roots for potential future trust adjustments.

## Who is affected

According to BleepingComputer, the emergency updates specifically target systems that do not receive automatic Certificate Trust List (CTL) updates, such as offline devices, environments without Internet connectivity, or systems where automatic certificate updates are disabled. The updates issued are KB5134133 for Windows 11 versions 24H2, 25H2, and 26H2, as well as KB5134125 for Windows 10 versions 21H2 and 22H2. Organisations relying on digital certificates issued under the affected eMudhra G3 roots or cross-signed hierarchies will also be impacted as trust is removed.

## Why it matters

The exposure of CA private keys presents a severe cyber security hazard. According to BleepingComputer, attackers possessing these compromised keys could forge legitimate-looking website certificates and code-signing signatures, enabling them to execute man-in-the-middle attacks, decrypt private traffic, or distribute malicious software disguised as trusted code.

Removing root certificates is a much broader action than revoking individual intermediate keys. While necessary to protect infrastructure, Microsoft highlighted that distrusting root certificates will break validation for valid child certificates issued under those hierarchies. Organisations operating in India and globally must evaluate whether their internal applications, encrypted services, or signed software depend on these eMudhra roots, as affected certificates will fail verification once updates are applied.

## What to do now

1. Install out-of-band updates KB5134133 or KB5134125 on disconnected, offline, or manually managed Windows 10 and Windows 11 systems, according to BleepingComputer.
1. Audit all enterprise web services, software packages, and internal systems to identify digital certificates that depend on the seven removed eMudhra root hierarchies.
1. Replace any active digital certificates linked to affected eMudhra G3 roots or cross-signs with valid alternatives from fully trusted certificate authorities.
1. Conduct an enterprise security assessment to evaluate whether local administrative policies require temporary local trust exceptions for business-critical applications.
1. Monitor future updates from Microsoft as the company continues investigating remaining eMudhra root certificates for further potential trust changes.

## Key takeaways

* Microsoft issued emergency updates to remove trust from seven eMudhra root certificates following severe key vulnerabilities, according to BleepingComputer.
* Offline and disconnected Windows 10 and 11 devices must be updated manually via out-of-band patches KB5134125 and KB5134133.
* IT teams must immediately audit and replace any organisational certificates dependent on the distrusted eMudhra root hierarchies.
